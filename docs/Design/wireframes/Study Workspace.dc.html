The Science of Deep Work

1 In the modern workplace, the ability to focus without distraction on a cognitively demanding task has become increasingly rare — and increasingly valuable. Cal Newport calls this skill deep work, and argues it is one of the most important capabilities a knowledge worker can develop.

2 The opposite of deep work is what Newport calls shallow work: non-cognitively demanding, logistical tasks often performed while distracted. Checking email, attending routine meetings, and scrolling through social media are all forms of shallow work that, while necessary, generate little lasting value.

3 To cultivate deep work, Newport suggests scheduling dedicated focus blocks — often two to four hours of uninterrupted time — and ruthlessly protecting them from interruption. This requires not just willpower, but structural changes to how one works.
